TITLE
Explainable Learned Fusion for Robust SMS Phishing Detection: A Statistically Validated Study of Smishing Filters

ABSTRACT (paste into the EDAS abstract box)
SMS phishing (smishing) reaches users on a personal channel where few filters and no explanations exist, and published detectors are often evaluated on a single split that mixes smishing with ordinary spam. We present the SMS engine of PhishSentry, which combines six interpretable heuristic detectors and a TF-IDF Naive Bayes model through a learned (stacked) fusion layer, and returns each verdict with the evidence behind it. On the public Mishra–Soni dataset (5,831 unique messages, 5×5-fold cross-validation, corrected significance tests), stacked fusion detects malicious SMS with 95.84% F1 at a 0.59% false-positive rate, +1.13 points over Naive Bayes alone (p = 0.015). Fixed-weight voting and a rule override, used in our earlier design, add nothing measurable. Separating smishing from commercial spam is harder (86.21% F1), yet 97.2% of those false alarms are spam and only 0.05% of personal messages are flagged. Leetspeak, homoglyph, split-letter and defanged-link obfuscation lower the stacked detector’s recall by at most 2.2 points, and a normalisation step recovers most of the loss. A verdict takes 0.45 ms on one CPU core.

KEYWORDS
smishing; SMS phishing; mobile security; ensemble learning; stacking; explainable AI; adversarial obfuscation
